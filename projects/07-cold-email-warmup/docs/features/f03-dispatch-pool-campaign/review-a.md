R1 — P2, AC-A4: equal timestamps defeat mailbox rotation. At `src/dispatch/store.ts:20`, the sole mailbox fairness key is `max(claimed_at)`; line24 writes the caller's unchanged `now`. Concurrent calls can capture the same millisecond before waiting for lock(7,1), and the supported deterministic clock seam can intentionally supply the same instant. After A and B have each been claimed at that instant, claiming A again does not advance its ordering key. With an older due backlog on A, subsequent claims keep choosing A until its quota/backlog is exhausted while B remains eligible. Thus the initial two-mailbox assertion passes without proving continued rotation.

Independent counterexample: PostgreSQL evaluated the exact ordering expression extracted from line20 against synthetic VALUES in `BEGIN READ ONLY`; after initial A/B claims, results were `third_claim|a2|A` and `fourth_claim|a3|A`, with B's due job still queued. No product table was read or mutated. This isolates the SQL ordering defect; it is not a full DispatchStore integration rerun. Full query/output: [astra-a-counterexample.txt](../../telemetry/features/20261002T211800Z-f03/astra-a-counterexample.txt).
